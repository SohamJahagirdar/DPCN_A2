## 1. Objective

The course maps the research output of 43+ Indian institutions (IITs, IIITs, NITs, central universities, IISc/TIFR/IISERs, CSIR and DAE labs). Each of the 30 groups maps its 2–3 assigned institutions. Group 18 covers **IISc Bangalore** and **TIFR Mumbai**.

The instruction note asks every group for four tasks:

1. **Data collection:** every faculty publication from 2016–2026, exported in a fixed CSV format.
2. **Statistics:** metrics at faculty, department and institution level: publication trend, Q1 fraction, h-index distribution, top journals, most-cited papers and research areas.
3. **Interactive dashboard:** at least the 11 panels listed in the instruction note, including two network visualisations.
4. **Network analysis:**
   - a citation network (papers → papers: in-degree, PageRank, citation flow with other Indian institutes);
   - an authorship network (faculty ↔ faculty: degree, betweenness, Louvain communities, cross-institution links);
   - an "Indian connectivity" map (partner institutes, cross-cluster links, topic similarity).

The analysis stays within our two institutions; links to other Indian institutes appear only in the raw files.

### 3.2 Pipeline
The pipeline is written in Python. `python run.py fetch`, then `python run.py build`.

1. **Fetch** (`pipeline/fetch.py`): downloads all 2016–2026 works affiliated with IISc and TIFR. Kept types: journal article, review, letter, book chapter, conference paper. Every API response is cached, so interrupted runs resume for free.
2. **Scope cleaning:** OpenAlex tags many authors at TIFR's *other* centres with the main TIFR ID. These are NCBS (Group 23), NCRA, ICTS, TIFR Hyderabad, TIFR-CAM and HBCSE. We detect them from their affiliation text and drop them, so "TIFR" means the **Mumbai campus**.
3. **Faculty identification** (`pipeline/build.py`):
   - Roster names are matched to OpenAlex author IDs. Matching handles initials and name order, e.g. "Rao M V Srisha" = "M. V. Srisha Rao".
   - Each match is checked against the department named in the paper's affiliation text, so two people with a common name ("Praveen Kumar") are not merged.
   - Former faculty who left or retired before the 2025-26 rosters are added by a rule: at least 8 papers at the institute over at least 4 different years, with at least 30% of them as last or corresponding author.
4. **Departments:** taken from the roster. For former faculty, the most frequent department in their affiliation text is used.
5. **Quality metrics:** SJR best quartile for journals and CORE rank for conferences. For each faculty member we compute h-index on their 2016–2026 papers at the institute, h-index without mega-collaboration papers, and OpenAlex's career h-index.
6. **Citation network** (`pipeline/networks.py`): a directed graph of our 42,725 papers, with edges taken from OpenAlex reference lists. We compute in-degree and PageRank (α = 0.85).
7. **Citation flow with other Indian institutes:**
   - *Out-flow:* papers by other Indian institutes that our papers cite. Collected as exact edges; this fills `cites_indian_inst`.
   - *In-flow:* papers by other Indian institutes that cite ours. Collected as exact edges; this fills `cited_by_indian_inst`.
   - Works that include our own institute are excluded from both, which removes self-citation.
8. **Authorship network:**
   - Raw file: every co-author pair on a paper with at most 50 authors, where at least one author is from our institutes and the other is from our institutes or any Indian institute.
   - Analysis graph: faculty-to-faculty, edge weight = joint papers. Degree and betweenness centrality, Louvain communities (seed 42) and modularity.
9. **Indian connectivity map:**
   - Partner institutes are ranked by shared papers.
   - Each is assigned a cluster following the course PPT: IIT, NIT, IIIT, IISc/TIFR/IISER, Premier Science, CSIR, DAE, Central University, plus Other DAE, Govt R&D, Medical, Industry and other universities. Partners outside our own cluster are flagged as cross-cluster.
   - Topic similarity is Jaccard on OpenAlex subfield profiles, computed for every partner with at least 10 shared papers. We report two versions: binary (presence of a subfield) and weighted by share of output.
   - Shared papers are given both with and without mega-collaborations (`shared_papers`, `shared_papers_excl_mega`).
10. **Large-team flag:** papers with more than 12 authors are flagged (`authors_gt_12`), as requested by the instructor. The dashboard's **Large-team papers** tab lists all of them (searchable, filterable, with a CSV download).
11. **With and without flagged papers** (`pipeline/variants.py`, instructor's request): after the main analysis, everything is recomputed without the flagged papers. This covers faculty metrics, citation network and PageRank, co-authorship communities, Indian citation flows and partners. The dashboard has an *included / excluded* switch and a *With vs without flagged* tab.
12. **Journal choice** (`pipeline/journals.py`, instructor's request). "High-impact" means OpenAlex 2-year mean citedness ≥ 10, the impact-factor equivalent.
    - *Who:* per faculty — journals used, loyalty (share of their papers in their own top-3 journals), Q1 and high-impact share. Aggregated by rank (2025-26 title) and department.
    - *Why:* share of papers in Q1 / high-impact journals by team size, collaboration type, field and rank. A logistic regression on faculty–paper pairs holds the other factors fixed (paper-clustered 95% CIs).
    - *How:* trends over time (high-impact, open access), publishers, and a journal co-usage network. Journals are linked by shared faculty; Louvain communities = publication ecosystems.
13. **Reputable journals and impact on India** (`pipeline/reputable.py`, instructor's request). See section 3.4 for every definition and its source.
14. **Impact on India beyond research** (`pipeline/impact.py`): patents, industry and start-ups, national missions and public services, case studies. See section 3.5.
15. **Network tests** (`pipeline/netstats.py`): the faculty network against 20 degree-preserving random rewirings (Maslov & Sneppen 2002): clustering, path length and small-world index, Louvain modularity z-score, degree and department/institute assortativity, rich club; cumulative network growth; a maximum-likelihood power-law fit of citation in-degree (Clauset et al. 2009).
16. **Statistics and dashboard** (`pipeline/stats.py`, `pipeline/dashboard.py`): all metrics go into one self-contained HTML file. The data and the chart libraries (Plotly, vis-network) are embedded, so the file works fully offline and can be sent as a single attachment. Tabs: Overview (key numbers, key findings, trend, research areas) · Impact on India · Reputable journals & India · Journal choice · Publications · Faculty · Large-team papers (with vs without flagged, full list) · Citation network · Co-authorship network · Indian connectivity · Method. Switches: IISc / TIFR / Both, flagged papers included / excluded, light / dark.

### 3.4 Reputable journals and impact on India: how it is measured

**Reputable journals.** These are the 178 journals and conference proceedings tracked by the **Nature Index** [1]. They were "selected based on reputation by panels of actively publishing scientists", independently of Springer Nature, and validated by a survey of researchers [1].
- The index's article rules are applied [1]. For Physical Review A, B and D only Letters count; we find them by the "L" in their DOI. Editors' Suggestions and highlighted articles also count in the index, but they are not identifiable in OpenAlex, so they are not counted here.
- For MNRAS only its Letters count. For Astronomy & Astrophysics only Letters count, which OpenAlex does not mark, so none are counted. This is a slight undercount.
- **Flagship** = Nature, Science, Cell and PNAS, a subset.
- 101 of the 178 Nature Index journals contain our papers.

**Impact on India.** All measures use OpenAlex work fields [2]. An "Indian institution" is one whose OpenAlex record has `country_code = IN`.

| # | Measure | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Indian citations (uptake) | Papers that cite ours and have at least one author at an Indian institution, excluding papers that involve the paper's own institute (self-citation) | `referenced_works` of the citing papers [2] |
| 2 | Indian share | Indian citations ÷ all citations | `cited_by_count` [2] |
| 3 | Breadth | Distinct Indian institutions citing the paper | institution IDs [2] |
| 4 | Reach beyond the elite | Cited by at least one state/private university or college (outside IIT, NIT, IIIT, IISc/TIFR/IISER, CSIR, DAE, central universities) | course PPT clusters |
| 5 | Speed | Years from publication to the first Indian citation | `publication_year` [2] |
| 6 | Indian Citation Index (ICI) | A paper's Indian citations ÷ the mean for our journal papers of the same field and year (1.0 = typical) | built like OpenAlex's FWCI [2] |
| 7 | Indian collaboration | At least one co-author at another Indian institution | authorships [2] |
| 8 | India-focused | Title or abstract names India, an Indian state or city, a region (Himalaya, Western Ghats, Ganga, ...) or a national facility or mission (AstroSat, GMRT, Chandrayaan, ...); affiliation phrases removed first | abstracts [2]; term list in `pipeline/fetch.py` |
| 9 | SDG relevance | UN Sustainable Development Goal tag with score ≥ 0.8 (tags at that level are correct about 7 times in 10) | OpenAlex SDG classifier [3]; UN goals [4] |
| – | Global benchmarks | Field-weighted citation impact (1.0 = world average for type, year and subfield) and top-10% flag | `fwci`, `citation_normalized_percentile` [2] |

**Limitations.**
- This section measures use by Indian *research*. Use outside research (patents, industry, government agencies) is in section 3.5.
- About 71% of papers have an abstract in OpenAlex; the rest are judged "India-focused" from the title only.

### 3.5 Impact on India beyond research: technology, economy, national missions

**What can and cannot be shown.** No dataset can prove that a paper *caused* economic growth or a better product. What bibliometrics can show are the documented *pathways* through which research reaches users outside academia [7][8]. Each pathway leaves a record (a patent, a co-authored paper, a citing organisation), so every number can be traced to its source.

| Pathway | Measure | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technology | Cited by patents | Patents worldwide citing the paper, at match confidence ≥ 4 (the dataset authors' own filter). Patent citations are the standard bibliometric link between science and technology [9][10] | Reliance on Science v64 [5][6] |
| Technology | Patents by our own authors | An author of the paper is an inventor on the citing patent (the dataset's `self` flag) | [5][6] |
| Economy | Industry co-authorship | At least one co-author at a company in India: OpenAlex institution type "company", plus company addresses (Pvt. Ltd., Limited, LLP) with an Indian location in the authors' affiliation text. Industry co-authorship is a standard university–industry indicator [11] | OpenAlex authorships [2] |
| Economy | Ownership | Public sector company (PSU), Indian private company, or multinational (foreign headquarters), from the company name | lists in `pipeline/impact.py` |
| Economy | Start-ups at IISc | Companies whose address is IISc's incubators (Society for Innovation and Development, CeNSE, CPDM) or campus | affiliation text [2] |
| Economy | Industry citations | Citing papers with an author at a company in India | OpenAlex [2] |
| Missions & public services | Sector co-authorship and citation | Co-authored with, or cited by, an Indian organisation of the sector: space, defence & aerospace, atomic energy, earth/climate/environment, agriculture, health & medicine, CSIR labs, government & policy, NGOs & think tanks (from the institution name and OpenAlex type). Citing papers involving our own institute are excluded | OpenAlex [2] |
| All | Case studies | Documented outcomes in India (products, licences, approvals, court orders, funded companies, national facilities), each checked against public sources and joined to the matching papers in our data. *Documented outcome* = the sources report a concrete result; *Partly documented* = the link or the result is only partly shown | `data/external/impact_case_studies.json` |

**Results (both institutes).**
- **Used outside academia:** 11,053 papers (25.9%) were co-authored or cited by an Indian company, government agency, hospital or NGO (IISc 27.3%, TIFR 23.6%).
- **Technology:** 732 papers published up to 2023 (2.4%) are cited by 1,411 patents in 16 patent offices (US 591, China 505, PCT 166, Europe 70). IISc 2.6%, TIFR 0.8%. The highest rates are in immunology and microbiology (6.2%), computer science (4.7%), chemistry (4.4%) and energy (4.2%). 31 patents name an author of the cited paper as inventor.
- **Economy:** 1,348 papers (3.2%; IISc 3.7%, TIFR 0.5%) have a co-author at one of 350 companies in India: multinational R&D centres (686 papers; Bosch 301, IBM, Microsoft Research, Samsung, Texas Instruments, Intel), Indian private companies (601; TCS, Tata Steel, Infosys, Mynvax) and public sector companies (90; Bharat Electronics, HAL, BHEL, Power Grid). 23 start-ups based at IISc's incubators or campus co-authored papers (for example Mynvax, AGNIT Semiconductors, INFAB Semiconductor, PathShodh Healthcare, Theranautilus, Quan2D). Tata Steel and TCS researchers cite our papers most.
- **National missions and public services** (papers co-authored / papers cited by the sector): atomic energy 1,387 / 1,221; health & medicine 880 / 1,726; CSIR labs 579 / 2,544; earth, climate & environment 548 / 1,197 (4,907 citing papers, the most of any sector: MoES, IITM, IMD, INCOIS); space 473 / 674; defence & aerospace 445 / 657; agriculture 174 / 705; government & policy 187 / 1,063.
- **Case studies (23; 15 documented outcomes):**
  - *Health:* IISc oxygen concentrators licensed to 24 companies (Karnataka ordered them for 2,508 public health centres); Strand Life Sciences' hereditary cancer tests; an ICMR-approved COVID-19 RT-PCR kit (Equine Biotech); Mynvax's heat-stable vaccine; Mumbai's COVID-19 sero-survey with BMC (TIFR); an antivenom partnership with Bharat Serums and Vaccines; PathShodh and Theranautilus devices.
  - *Technology & industry:* AGNIT (gallium-nitride chips), Simyog (EMC software), Morphing Machines (reconfigurable processor), INFAB (MEMS), the Bosch-funded cyber-physical systems centre, Satyukt (satellite advice for farmers), Vaani (open speech data for India's languages).
  - *National missions:* IISc's National Quantum Mission quantum-computing hub; TIFR qubits in DRDO's 6-qubit processor; TIFR's AstroSat instruments; TIFR's part in the gravitational-wave discoveries and LIGO-India.
  - *Environment & policy:* IISc wetland studies used by the National Green Tribunal (75 m lake buffers, ₹500 crore escrow); IISc's national climate-vulnerability framework; monsoon and fog research used by forecasters.
- **Reputable journals:** Nature Index papers are cited by patents more often than our other journal papers (3.1% vs 2.2%) and used outside academia more often (37.5% vs 28.9%), but rarely have industry co-authors (0.8% vs 2.6%). India-focused Nature Index papers reach non-academic users most often (74.8%).

**Limitations.**
- The patent data has no patents from the Indian Patent Office (its sources are full-text collections without India) and no assignees, so patents by Indian companies cannot be singled out. It ends in 2023, so recent papers are undercounted.
- Only a minority of Indian companies are in the institution registry OpenAlex uses; the address-text search finds more, but start-ups that do not write "Pvt. Ltd." or "Limited" are missed.
- Revenue, jobs, products sold and policy citations are not measured: no open data exists for Indian organisations (policy-citation databases such as Overton are commercial).
- Co-authorship and citation show use, not benefit. The case studies document outcomes, but they are examples found by searching public sources, not a complete list; some outcomes (the oxygen concentrator, the RT-PCR kit) have no paper in our data.

## 5. Known limitations

- **OpenAlex coverage:** affiliations and references are not complete. Some papers have no venue (about 0.4%), and roughly 4% of journal papers have no SJR quartile.
- **Long author lists:** the OpenAlex list API shows at most 100 authors per paper. For the 1,435 affected papers the full author list was fetched with single-work lookups, so author counts and our authors are complete. Papers where even the full list shows no identifiable IISc/TIFR author are removed as OpenAlex mis-tags (1 paper).
- **Faculty lists are current (2025-26):** former faculty come from the publication rule above, which can include a few long-term postdocs or research scientists. They are marked `faculty_source = heuristic`.
- **Name matching:** 19 roster entries (mostly new hires with no papers at the institute yet) are not matched; they are listed as "roster (no OpenAlex match)". Authors without an OpenAlex author ID are matched by name. 13 such faculty have no OpenAlex profile, so their lifetime h-index is blank.

## 6. Design decisions and assumptions

These are the definitions we chose where the instruction note leaves room for interpretation. Each can be changed in `config.py` or one function, followed by a rebuild.

| Topic | Our choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| TIFR scope | **Mumbai campus only.** Authors at NCBS, NCRA, ICTS, TIFR Hyderabad, TIFR-CAM and HBCSE are recognised from their affiliation text and excluded, even when OpenAlex gives them the main TIFR ID. | The course assigns "TIFR Mumbai", and NCBS belongs to Group 23. |
| Publication types | Journal article, review, letter, book chapter, conference paper. arXiv-only preprints are excluded. | Counts peer-reviewed output only and avoids double counting with published versions. |
| Faculty | Official 2025-26 rosters, plus former faculty identified by a publication rule (§3.2). | The rosters are current lists, but the study covers 2016–2026. |
| Dataset rows | One row per paper × institution × faculty author. Papers without a matched faculty author get one row (`is_faculty = FALSE`). | Matches the "author" column of the template; every paper is still present. |
| Large-team flag | `authors_gt_12` = TRUE if the paper has more than 12 authors. Flagged papers stay in all counts; the dashboard shows them per year, compares their citations and lists them all (Large-team papers tab). | Instructor's request (updated 15 Oct 2026: > 12 authors, because papers with 11–12 authors are common). Threshold is `AUTHOR_FLAG_THRESHOLD` in `config.py`. |
| Mega-collaborations (> 50 authors) | Kept in the dataset and publication counts (flag `mega_collaboration`). Excluded from the co-authorship network. h-index reported with and without them. | CMS/ALICE-style author lists would otherwise dominate the networks. |
| h-index | Shown in three versions: 2016–2026 papers at the institute, the same without mega-collaborations, and OpenAlex lifetime h-index. | The template does not specify which one. |
| Quartile | SCImago best quartile of the journal in the publication year; 2026 papers use SJR 2025. For conference papers, the CORE 2023 rank. | Matches the Q1–Q4 / A*–C scheme in the template. |
| `cites_indian_inst` / `cited_by_indian_inst` | TRUE if the paper cites, or is cited by, a paper with an author at another Indian institution. Works that include the row's own institution are not counted (removes self-citation). | Captures cross-institute links only. |
| In-flow | Collected as exact citation edges (`--steps inflow`, about 1,400 API calls), so `cited_by_indian_inst` is filled per paper. A cheaper aggregate mode (`inflow_agg`) is also available. | Exact per-paper flags, as the template requires. |
| `total_citations` (indian_connections) | Total citations of the papers co-authored with the partner. Citations to and from the partner are separate columns. | The template does not define it. |
| Partner clusters | PPT clusters (IIT, NIT, IIIT, IISc/TIFR/IISER, Premier Science, CSIR, DAE, Central University), plus Other DAE, Govt R&D/Autonomous, Medical, Industry and other universities. Anything outside IISc/TIFR/IISER counts as cross-cluster. | Follows the course's grouping. |
